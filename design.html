STEP 03 — DESIGN

Turn your manuscript into a real-looking book.

Learn how to design your cover, choose a book size, format your pages, select fonts, create chapter layouts, add page numbers, prepare front and back matter and make your final files ready for publishing.

THE COMPLETE PROCESS

Designing a book from start to finish

Follow these steps in order. This prevents many common formatting problems later.

01

Choose your book size

Decide the trim size before designing your interior.

02

Format the interior

Set margins, fonts, spacing, headings and page numbers.

03

Design the cover

Create the front, spine and back cover when required.

04

Add book matter

Prepare the title page, copyright page, contents and other sections.

05

Export correctly

Create the final PDF or other required publishing files.

06

Check everything

Proofread, inspect the PDF and check the printed result before ordering many copies.

01 — BOOK SIZE

Choose the right trim size.

Trim size is the final physical size of the printed page after the book has been trimmed.

Book Type Possible Size Good For
Compact Novel 5.5 × 8.5 in Novels and fiction
Standard Novel 6 × 9 in Novels, nonfiction
Workbook 8.5 × 11 in Exercises and worksheets
Square Book 8.5 × 8.5 in Illustrated books
Children's Book Depends on format Picture-heavy books
Important: Do not randomly choose a cover size before deciding the final trim size and page count. Your complete print cover dimensions can depend on these specifications.

02 — INTERIOR

Make your pages comfortable to read.

Good interior design should feel almost invisible. Readers should notice the story—not fight with the formatting.

Margins

Leave enough space around the text so pages don't feel cramped.

Line Spacing

Avoid extremely tight or extremely loose lines.

Paragraphs

Use a consistent paragraph style throughout the manuscript.

Headings

Chapter headings should follow one consistent design system.

Page Numbers

Place them consistently and decide which opening pages should display numbers.

White Space

Empty space is useful. Don't try to fill every part of every page.

03 — FONTS

Choose fonts readers can actually read.

Decorative fonts can look great on a cover but may be difficult to read across hundreds of pages.

Body Text

Choose a readable serif or sans-serif typeface and keep it consistent.

Example:

A readable body font should make a full page comfortable to read rather than demanding attention from every letter.

Chapter Titles

Chapter headings can have more personality, but they should still fit the overall design.

Cover Title

Your cover title should be readable even when the cover is viewed as a small thumbnail.

Don't Overdo It

Using many unrelated fonts can make a book look inconsistent. A simple font system usually looks more professional.

Simple Font System

Book Title

Large display style

Chapter Title

Medium heading style

Body

Highly readable paragraph style

04 — CHAPTERS

Create a consistent chapter layout.

Every chapter doesn't need to look identical to a poster. A simple, repeatable structure is often best.

CHAPTER 1

The Beginning

The first paragraph begins after the chapter heading. Continue with the same body-text style throughout the book.

Use consistent paragraph spacing and indentation.

Option A

Large chapter number + title.

Option B

Simple centered chapter title.

Option C

Chapter number + small decorative element + title.

05 — FRONT MATTER

What comes before Chapter 1?

Depending on the book, you may include several preliminary pages.

01

Half Title

Optional page containing the title.

02

Title Page

Usually includes the title, subtitle if applicable, and author name.

03

Copyright Page

Contains copyright and publication information.

04

Dedication

Optional short dedication to someone.

05

Table of Contents

Useful especially for nonfiction, educational and reference books.

06 — BACK MATTER

What comes after the final chapter?

About the Author

A short author biography.

Acknowledgements

Thank people who helped with the book.

Preview

You may include a preview or excerpt of another work.

Other Books

List other books by the author.

Author Website

Provide a website or other appropriate contact information.

Notes / References

Useful for nonfiction and educational books.

07 — COVER

Understand the three parts of a print cover.

📕

Front

Usually contains the title, author name and main visual.

📖

Spine

The narrow area between the front and back cover. Spine text may depend on the book's specifications.

📘

Back

Can contain a description, author information, publisher information and other appropriate elements.

Never guess the full cover size. For print-on-demand books, generate the correct template from the publishing platform using your exact trim size, page count, paper and binding specifications.
08 — FREE DESIGN TOOLS

Make a cover without expensive software.

Here are useful beginner-friendly tools and exactly what to do with them.

🎨 Canva — Book Covers

Canva provides editable book-cover templates and also lets you start from a blank design.

How to use it:

  1. Open the book-cover template page.
  2. Search for your genre—for example: mystery, fantasy, thriller, poetry or children's book.
  3. Choose a design that matches your story.
  4. Replace the sample title with your title.
  5. Replace the author name.
  6. Change colours and images.
  7. Make sure the title remains readable.
  8. Download the final design in the format required by your publishing platform.
Open Canva Book Covers →

How to use it:

  3. Add your title.
  4. Add your author name.
  5. Upload your own image or use available design resources.
  6. Adjust fonts, spacing and colours.
  7. Review the design at both full size and thumbnail size.
  8. Export the finished design.

09 — IMAGES

Need images for your cover?

Don't simply take random images from Google Images. Check the licence of the image source and the specific image when necessary.

📷 Pexels

A source of free photos and videos.

How to use it:

  1. Search for a visual matching your story.
  2. Try descriptive searches instead of your exact book title.
  3. Download a suitable image.
  4. Import it into your cover editor.
  5. Combine it with your own typography and design.
Visit Pexels →

Pexels states that its photos and videos can be downloaded and used for free, including commercial use, subject to its licence and restrictions.

📸 Unsplash

Another source for high-quality photography.

How to use it:

  1. Search for the mood or subject of your book.
  2. Choose an image with enough empty space for your title.
  3. Download the image.
  4. Add it to your cover design.
  5. Check the current Unsplash licence and any rights issues involving recognizable people, brands or artwork.
Visit Unsplash →

Unsplash's current licence allows broad free use, including commercial use, but its terms contain important restrictions for trademarks, recognizable people and works of authorship.

10 — COLOUR

Choose colours that fit the story.

Mystery

Darker tones, strong contrast and limited accent colours can create a mysterious visual mood.

Fantasy

Rich colours, atmospheric backgrounds and distinctive accent colours can help establish a fantastical mood.

Educational

Clear contrast and restrained colours can make information easier to navigate.

  4. Use the same colours consistently in your cover or promotional graphics.

11 — COVER FORMULA

A simple cover-building formula.

1. Title

Make it clear and readable.

2. Visual

Choose an image, illustration, shape or visual concept that supports the story.

3. Author

Place the author name where it is easy to find without overpowering the title.

Example

Imagine a mystery novel called The Silent Door.

Instead of filling the cover with ten different images, you might use one strong doorway image, a restrained colour palette, a readable title and a clear author name.

12 — BACK COVER

What should your back cover do?

The back cover should give the reader a reason to want to know more without explaining the entire story.

Start with the hook

Introduce the central situation or question.

Introduce the stakes

What could the protagonist gain or lose?

Create curiosity

Leave the reader with a question they want answered.

Don't reveal everything

Avoid explaining the ending or major twists.

Simple Back-Cover Structure

Hook → Problem → Stakes → Mystery → Invitation to read

13 — PAGE NUMBERS

Keep pagination consistent.

Placement

Choose a consistent location such as the outside footer or header area.

Consistency

Don't move page numbers randomly between chapters.

Opening Pages

Decide which front-matter pages should display numbers according to your chosen publishing format.

Tip: Use your word processor's automatic page-numbering tools instead of manually typing numbers onto every page.

15 — AVOID THESE

Common design mistakes.

❌ Too many fonts

Keep the typography system simple.

❌ Tiny text

Don't sacrifice readability just to fit more words.

❌ Random margins

Keep your page structure consistent.

❌ Manual spaces everywhere

Use proper formatting tools instead of hundreds of spaces and blank lines.

❌ Low-quality images

Use suitable high-resolution artwork and follow the requirements of your printer or publisher.

❌ Guessing the cover dimensions

Generate the appropriate template from your publishing platform whenever possible.

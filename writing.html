STEP 01 — WRITING

Turn an idea into a real story.

You don't need to know how to write a perfect book before starting. You need an idea, a process and the willingness to improve one page at a time.

01 — THE IDEA

Every book starts with a question.

A story idea does not have to be complicated. Start with something interesting and ask, "What if?"

💡

The What-If Method

Take an ordinary situation and change one important thing.

Example

What if a student discovered that every electrical device in his city stopped working whenever he touched it?

🔍

The Question Method

Start with a question that makes someone want to know the answer.

Example

Why does an abandoned house have a light switched on every night even though nobody lives there?

Quick rule: If your idea makes you curious about what happens next, it probably has the beginning of a story.
02 — THE PREMISE

Turn the idea into one clear sentence.

A premise is the simple foundation of your story. It helps you stay focused when the story becomes complicated.

The Premise Formula

When something happens, a main character must achieve something before something goes wrong.

Example

When a mysterious storm gives a teenager control over electricity, he must learn to control his new ability before a dangerous enemy uses the same phenomenon against his city.

Who?

Who is the story about?

Wants What?

What does the character want to achieve?

Why Is It Difficult?

What obstacle prevents them from getting it?

03 — GENRE

Know what kind of story you're building.

Genre gives readers an expectation about the kind of experience your book will provide.

🕵️

Mystery

A question, secret or puzzle must be solved.

⚡

Thriller

High stakes, danger, urgency and escalating tension.

🐉

Fantasy

Imaginary worlds, magical systems or extraordinary creatures.

🚀

Science Fiction

Stories built around science, technology or possible futures.

😂

Comedy

Humour and entertaining situations drive the experience.

📖

Adventure

Characters face challenges while exploring places, situations or discoveries.

04 — CHARACTER

Build someone readers can follow.

A character becomes interesting when they want something and something stands in their way.

🎯

Character Goal

What does the character want?

Example

"I want to find my missing brother."

🚧

Character Obstacle

What prevents the character from reaching the goal?

Example

"Someone is deliberately hiding information from me."

❤️

Character Motivation

Why does the character care about the goal?

🔄

Character Change

How does the experience change the character by the end?

05 — IMAGINATION

How do you actually imagine a character?

Don't begin by trying to create a perfect biography. Imagine the person in situations and observe how they behave.

☀️

Put Them in an Ordinary Day

What do they do when they wake up? What do they enjoy? What annoys them?

⚠️

Give Them a Problem

Imagine the character suddenly facing a difficult situation. What is their first reaction?

🗣️

Hear Their Voice

Imagine how they speak. Are they confident, nervous, sarcastic, quiet or energetic?

Character imagination exercise

Your character finds a wallet containing money and an identification card. They are alone. Nobody is watching. What do they do?

The answer can reveal personality, values, fears and weaknesses without directly explaining them to the reader.

06 — PLOT

Give your story somewhere to go.

You don't have to know every sentence before writing. But knowing the major turning points can keep the story moving.

01

Beginning

Introduce the character, their world and the situation before the major problem appears.

02

Inciting Event

Something happens that changes the direction of the character's life.

03

Rising Problems

Attempts to solve the problem create new complications.

04

Turning Point

A major discovery, decision or event changes what the character must do.

05

Climax

The central conflict reaches its most important moment.

06

Resolution

Show the consequences and where the characters end up.

07 — EXAMPLE

See the structure in action.

Stage Story Event
Beginning A student discovers a strange device in an old laboratory.
Inciting Event The device activates and sends a mysterious signal.
Rising Problems Strange events begin happening around the city.
Turning Point The student discovers that someone is searching for the device.
Climax The student must decide whether to destroy the device or use it to stop the threat.
Resolution The immediate danger ends, but the device leaves behind a new mystery.
08 — CHAPTERS

Plan your chapters without overcomplicating them.

Each chapter should do something. It can reveal information, create a problem, change a relationship or move the plot forward.

09 — SCENES

Build chapters from meaningful scenes.

A scene works best when something changes between its beginning and its ending.

🎯

Goal

What does the character want in this scene?

🚧

Conflict

What makes achieving that goal difficult?

🔄

Change

What is different by the end of the scene?

Example

A character enters a library wanting to find an old map. The librarian refuses to help. The character searches alone and discovers that the map has already been removed. They leave with a new clue instead.

10 — DIALOGUE

Make characters sound like people.

Dialogue should not simply repeat information the reader already knows. It should reveal character, create conflict or move the story forward.

Weak Dialogue

"Hello, Rahul. How are you?"

"I am fine. I am going to the library because I need to find a book."

More Purposeful Dialogue

"You're going to the library again?"

"I have to find something."

"The map?"

Rahul stopped.

Remember: People don't always say exactly what they think. Sometimes what a character avoids saying is just as important as what they say.
11 — DESCRIPTION

Show important moments through action.

"Show, don't tell" does not mean you can never explain anything. It means that important moments can often become stronger when readers experience them through details.

Telling

Arjun was nervous.

Showing

Arjun read the message three times before his thumb finally moved toward the reply button.

12 — PACING

Control how quickly the story feels.

Pacing is not simply about making everything fast. Different moments need different amounts of space.

⚡

Fast Pacing

Shorter paragraphs, action, urgent decisions and immediate consequences can create speed.

🌙

Slow Pacing

Reflection, atmosphere, description and character development can give important moments room to breathe.

🎚️

Variation

Strong stories usually change pace instead of staying at maximum intensity all the time.

13 — SUSPENSE

Make readers ask, "What happens next?"

Suspense can come from unanswered questions, time pressure, incomplete information and difficult choices.

Ask a Question

Introduce something the reader wants explained.

Example

Why is the same unknown symbol appearing everywhere?

Create a Deadline

Give the character limited time to solve a problem.

Example

The character has until sunrise to discover the truth.

Hide Information

Give the reader enough information to become curious, without immediately explaining everything.

Force a Choice

Put the character between two difficult options.

14 — ROUTINE

Actually finish the manuscript.

A brilliant idea doesn't become a book until you turn it into pages.

01

Choose a realistic target

Decide how often you can realistically write.

02

Separate drafting from editing

Don't stop every few sentences to make everything perfect.

03

Keep notes

Record ideas, names, plot points and questions so you don't lose them.

04

Finish the first draft

The first draft's job is to exist. Refinement comes later.

15 — EDITING

Don't edit everything at once.

Use separate passes so each edit has a clear purpose.

🧩

Story Pass

Does the plot make sense? Are there missing events?

🎭

Character Pass

Are characters consistent? Do their decisions make sense?

✏️

Language Pass

Improve sentences, word choice and clarity.

🔎

Proofreading

Check spelling, punctuation, formatting and small errors.

Important: Don't try to make every sentence perfect while writing your first draft. That can make finishing much harder.

STEP 02 — CHARACTERS

Create characters readers remember.

A great character is more than a name, age and appearance. Give your characters goals, fears, strengths, weaknesses, relationships, secrets and reasons to make the choices they make.

QUICK START

What makes a character interesting?

Start with these six foundations.

🎯

Goal

What does the character want?

❤️

Motivation

Why do they want it?

🚧

Obstacle

What is stopping them?

😨

Fear

What are they afraid of losing or facing?

💪

Strength

What makes them capable?

🔄

Change

How are they different at the end?

01 — CHARACTER TYPES

Know the role each character plays.

Not every character needs the same amount of development. Their importance depends on their role in the story.

⭐

Protagonist

The main character whose journey forms the central focus of the story.

⚔️

Antagonist

A person, force or situation that creates major opposition for the protagonist.

🤝

Supporting Character

Helps develop the protagonist, plot, world or conflict.

🧑‍🏫

Mentor

Provides knowledge, guidance, training or another perspective.

🪞

Foil

A character whose differences highlight another character's qualities.

🎭

Minor Character

Appears briefly to support a particular scene or purpose.

02 — IMAGINATION

How do you actually imagine a character?

Instead of filling out random facts, imagine your character behaving in different situations.

01

Imagine their morning

What do they do after waking up? Are they organised, lazy, energetic, nervous or calm?

02

Give them a difficult choice

Put them between two options and decide what they would choose.

03

Give them a secret

What do they know that other characters don't?

04

Give them a contradiction

Perhaps a brave person is afraid of disappointing others, or a confident person secretly doubts themselves.

🧠 Imagination Exercise

Your character discovers a lost wallet containing money. Nobody is watching.

Ask yourself:

  • Do they keep it?
  • Do they try to find the owner?
  • Do they hesitate?
  • What makes them choose?
  • Would their decision change if someone were watching?
03 — APPEARANCE

Design the character visually.

Appearance should support the character rather than becoming a giant list of physical details.

Face

Think about expressions and how emotions appear on their face.

Hair

Style and how the character treats their hair can contribute to personality.

Clothing

Clothing can show occupation, personality, environment or personal taste.

Movement

Do they walk quickly? Avoid eye contact? Stand confidently?

Expressions

Think about what their face does when they're happy, angry, nervous or surprised.

Distinctive Detail

Give them one or two memorable details readers can associate with them.

Writing tip: You don't need to describe every physical feature when the character first appears. Reveal useful details naturally through the story.
04 — PERSONALITY

Give your character a personality beyond adjectives.

Instead of simply writing "brave" or "funny", show how those qualities affect the character's decisions and behaviour.

Personality Questions

  • Are they optimistic or pessimistic?
  • Are they patient or impatient?
  • Do they trust people easily?
  • How do they react to criticism?
  • How do they behave under pressure?
  • What makes them laugh?
  • What annoys them?
  • What are they proud of?

Personality in Action

Instead of:

"Maya was brave."

Show:

"Maya knew the door might be locked, but she reached for the handle anyway."
05 — GOALS

Give every important character something they want.

Goals create direction. Without a goal, characters can feel like they are simply being carried through the plot.

🎯

External Goal

Something the character wants to achieve in the outside world.

Find the missing artifact.
❤️

Internal Goal

Something the character needs emotionally or personally.

Learn to trust others.
⏳

Immediate Goal

What they want right now in a particular scene.

Escape the room.
06 — FLAWS

Perfect characters are usually boring.

Weaknesses create opportunities for conflict and growth.

😨

Fear

What situation would make this character uncomfortable, uncertain or frightened?

⚠️

Flaw

What part of their personality causes problems?

💥

Mistake

What poor decision could they make because of their flaw?

🌱

Growth

What can they learn from the consequences?

Example Character Arc

Beginning: A character refuses to trust anyone.

Problem: Their refusal causes them to lose an important ally.

Realisation: They understand that accepting help isn't weakness.

End: They choose to trust someone when it matters.

08 — PROTAGONIST

Build a strong protagonist.

Your protagonist doesn't have to be perfect, powerful or constantly successful. They need to be worth following.

They Want Something

Give them a clear objective.

They Face Opposition

Make achieving their goal difficult.

They Make Choices

The protagonist should influence the story rather than simply react to everything.

They Make Mistakes

Mistakes create consequences and opportunities for growth.

They Learn

The journey should affect what they believe or understand.

They Decide

Important choices near the climax should matter.

09 — ANTAGONIST

Build an antagonist with a reason.

A strong antagonist should create meaningful opposition. They don't have to be evil simply because the story says so.

What do they want?

Give the antagonist a goal of their own.

Why do they want it?

Create motivation behind their actions.

What do they believe?

Give them a worldview that explains their decisions.

What makes them dangerous?

Their advantage could come from intelligence, influence, resources, knowledge or another ability.

Villain Example

A scientist wants to control a city's energy system. He believes centralised control will prevent future disasters. His goal may be understandable, but the methods he chooses create the central conflict.

10 — RELATIONSHIPS

Characters become richer through relationships.

Different relationships can reveal completely different sides of the same character.

🤝

Friendship

Trust, loyalty, humour, disagreements and shared memories.

⚔️

Rivalry

Competition can reveal ambition, insecurity and pride.

🧑‍🏫

Mentor

Can challenge the protagonist's beliefs and help them grow.

🏠

Family

Family relationships can create emotional stakes and personal history.

❓

Stranger

A mysterious character can introduce uncertainty or new information.

🪞

Mirror

Another character can represent what the protagonist might become—or what they fear becoming.

11 — SHOW, DON'T TELL

Don't just name the emotion. Let readers see it.

Instead of simply telling readers that a character is nervous, angry, excited or sad, describe their actions, expressions and behaviour so readers can experience the emotion.

😰 Nervous

❌ Telling

She was nervous.

✅ Showing

She kept fidgeting with the edge of her sleeve. Her fingers wouldn't stay still, and she glanced at the clock for what felt like the tenth time.

😟 Anxious

❌ Telling

She was anxious about the exam.

✅ Showing

She read the same question again and again. Her leg bounced beneath the desk as she tightened her grip on the pencil.

😠 Angry

❌ Telling

He was angry.

✅ Showing

He clenched his fists and stared at the floor. When he finally looked up, his voice was unusually quiet.

🤩 Excited

❌ Telling

She was excited.

✅ Showing

She could barely sit still. She checked the door every few seconds and smiled the moment she heard footsteps outside.

😔 Sad

❌ Telling

He was sad.

✅ Showing

He stared at the empty chair for a long moment. When someone spoke to him, he nodded without looking up.

😨 Scared

❌ Telling

She was scared.

✅ Showing

She froze beside the doorway. Her breathing became shallow as she slowly reached for the light switch.

😳 Embarrassed

❌ Telling

He was embarrassed.

✅ Showing

His face warmed. He looked away and suddenly became very interested in the floor.

😎 Confident

❌ Telling

He was confident.

✅ Showing

He walked into the room without hesitating, met everyone's eyes and calmly took his seat.

🤔 Confused

❌ Telling

She was confused.

✅ Showing

She looked from the map to the doorway, then back at the map. She slowly turned it upside down.

😞 Disappointed

❌ Telling

He was disappointed.

✅ Showing

His smile disappeared. He quietly folded the letter and slipped it back into his pocket.

😌 Relieved

❌ Telling

She was relieved.

✅ Showing

Her shoulders finally dropped. She let out a long breath and leaned back against the wall.

🔎 Curious

❌ Telling

He was curious.

✅ Showing

He leaned closer to the strange machine. His eyes moved over every button and hidden panel.

🧠 The Show-Don't-Tell Formula

When you want to show an emotion, ask these three questions:

1. What does the body do?

Fidgeting, clenched fists, shaking hands, looking away, smiling, freezing, pacing, tense shoulders...

2. What does the character do?

Avoids someone, checks the clock, walks away, laughs, hesitates, hides something...

3. What changes?

Voice, breathing, posture, expression, movement or behaviour can reveal the emotion.

Important: "Show, don't tell" is a useful technique, not an absolute rule. Sometimes directly stating an emotion is clearer and more effective—especially when the detail is unimportant. Use description when it adds something to the scene.

✍️ Your Turn

Rewrite these sentences without directly naming the emotion.

  • She was nervous before the presentation.
  • He was extremely angry with his friend.
  • Maya was excited about the surprise.
  • Rahul was scared when he heard the noise.
  • She was embarrassed after making the mistake.
12 — CHARACTER VOICE

Make every important character sound different.

Character voice includes vocabulary, sentence length, attitude, humour, confidence and what the character chooses to talk about.

Character A

Speaks quickly, uses short sentences and avoids emotional subjects.

"We need to go. Now."

Character B

Speaks carefully and tends to explain their reasoning.

"Before we leave, I think we should understand exactly what we're walking into."
Test: Remove character names from a dialogue scene. If you can still tell who is speaking, your character voices are probably distinct.
13 — CHARACTER ARC

Decide how your character changes.

A character arc is the journey from who a character is at the beginning to who they become by the end.

01

Starting Belief

What does the character believe at the beginning?

02

Challenge

What events challenge that belief?

03

Crisis

What moment forces them to make an important choice?

04

New Understanding

What do they finally understand?

05

Final Choice

How does their final decision demonstrate their growth?

14 — CHARACTER TESTS

Put your character under pressure.

These exercises can reveal personality faster than a long biography.

Test 1 — Failure

Your character fails at something extremely important. What do they do next?

Test 2 — Success

Your character gets exactly what they wanted. What happens after that?

Test 3 — Betrayal

Someone they trusted betrays them. How do they react?

Test 4 — Impossible Choice

They must choose between two things they deeply care about. What do they choose—and why?

15 — INTRODUCING CHARACTERS

Make the first appearance count.

When a major character enters the story, you don't need to dump their entire biography onto the reader.

❌ Information Dump

Aarav was sixteen years old. He had black hair, brown eyes and lived in Lucknow. He liked science, cricket and reading books. He had a younger sister and was usually quiet.

✅ Introduce Through Action

Aarav tightened the final screw and stepped back. His younger sister had already warned him not to touch the machine again.

He smiled.

He reached for the switch.

Why it works: The second version introduces personality and curiosity through behaviour. The reader can discover the character instead of receiving a list of facts.
16 — COMPLETE EXAMPLE

Example character: Aarav

Here's how the different pieces can fit together.

CHARACTER PROFILE

Aarav Mehta

A curious student who discovers a hidden machine beneath an abandoned building.

Goal Understand the machine
Fear Causing harm to others
Strength Curiosity and persistence
Weakness Acts before thinking
Secret He caused an earlier accident
Arc Learning responsibility

Why this works

Aarav's curiosity helps him discover the machine, but that same curiosity can create problems. His strength and weakness are connected, which gives the writer opportunities for natural conflict and growth.
